Core aeration is the single most effective thing you can do for a thin lawn in the Twin Cities. Most of the metro sits on clay that packs down under foot traffic, mowers, and months of snow load.

## Signs your lawn needs aeration

- Water puddles or runs off instead of soaking in.
- A screwdriver is hard to push more than a couple of inches into moist soil.
- The lawn thins out every summer no matter how much you feed it.
- There is a spongy layer of thatch more than half an inch thick.

## Why late summer beats spring

The best window is late August through September.

- **Warm soil, cool air.** Seed germinates fast and new roots grow without heat stress.
- **Fewer weeds.** Crabgrass is finishing its life cycle, so new grass does not have to compete.
- **A full fall to establish.** New grass has two months to root in before winter.

Spring aeration works, but it breaks up the crabgrass pre-emergent barrier. If you aerate in spring, skip the pre-emergent or use one that is safe for new seed.

## Always overseed afterward

Aeration opens thousands of small holes, which are perfect seed beds. Overseeding right behind the aerator is how you turn a thin lawn into a thick one. Use a Kentucky bluegrass and perennial rye blend for sun, or add fine fescue for shade.

## Leave the plugs

The soil cores look messy for a week or two, then break down and return soil and microbes to the surface. Do not rake them up.
