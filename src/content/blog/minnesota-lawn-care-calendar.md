Minnesota lawns grow in a short window. Get the timing right and a lawn can go from thin to thick in a single season. Get it wrong and the same money disappears into weeds and snow mold.

Here is the calendar our crews follow for Kentucky bluegrass lawns across the northwest metro.

## April: clean up and wake it up

Wait until the ground is firm before you walk on it. Raking soggy turf pulls out healthy crowns along with the dead grass.

- Rake out snow mold, the gray or pink matted patches, so air can reach the crown.
- Clear branches and debris, and re-edge the beds.
- Apply crabgrass pre-emergent when soil temperatures reach about 55 degrees. Forsythia blooming is a reliable sign.

## May: first mow and first feed

- Start mowing once the grass is actively growing, usually late April or early May.
- Keep the deck at 3 inches. Never remove more than a third of the blade in one cut.
- Put down a slow-release feed and spot treat dandelions while they are flowering.

## June to August: protect it from the heat

- Raise the cut to 3.5 inches in July. Taller grass shades the soil and holds moisture.
- Water deeply once or twice a week instead of a little every day.
- Apply grub prevention in June if you had beetle damage last year.

## September: the most important month

This is when next year's lawn is built. Warm soil and cool nights are ideal for new grass.

- Core aerate and overseed in late August through mid-September.
- Feed with a recovery fertilizer to push root growth.
- Treat creeping Charlie and other broadleaf weeds while they are pulling energy down into their roots.

## October and November: put it to bed

- Keep leaves off the grass. A wet mat over winter smothers it.
- Apply a winterizer after the last mow but before the ground freezes.
- Drop the final cut to about 2.5 inches to discourage snow mold and voles.
